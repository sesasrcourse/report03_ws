In Task 1, the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) algorithm was tested in a Gazebo simulation environment using the original cost function (see \autoref{sssec:dwa_cost}). The robot was required to follow a moving goal while avoiding static obstacles in the environment.

The results shown in \autoref{tab:metrics} demonstrate effective navigation performance with the baseline DWA implementation. The robot achieved a 100\% success rate across all trials, maintaining continuous tracking of the moving target throughout the entire experiment duration. As visible in \autoref{fig:s1_tr}, the robot trajectory closely follows the dynamic goal path, indicating proper heading alignment through the cost function.

The distance RMSE of 0.313~m indicates that the robot tends to follow the target keeping a certain distance from it. While the bearing RMSE of 0.133~rad confirms that the heading objective effectively maintains the target within the robot's field of view. 

Regarding obstacle avoidance, a conservative collision stop tolerance was maintained throughout the simulation experiments. Analysis of the dynamic goal trajectory revealed that higher tolerance values could cause the robot to become trapped against walls due to the velocity commands generated during target pursuit. The average obstacle distance of 1.905~m demonstrates safe navigation behavior throughout the experiment, while the minimum distance of 0.172~m indicates occasional proximity to obstacles, particularly when navigating narrow passages. Importantly, all recorded distances remain above the defined collision tolerance threshold, confirming the effectiveness of the obstacle avoidance term in the cost function.

The velocity command profiles (\autoref{fig:s1_cmd}) generated by the DWA exhibit noticeable jitter, especially in angular velocity. This behavior is characteristic of DWA implementations that optimize for instantaneous trajectory scoring without explicit smoothness constraints. While acceptable in simulation, such oscillations could be problematic on real hardware due to actuator limitations and dynamics not modeled in the algorithm.
