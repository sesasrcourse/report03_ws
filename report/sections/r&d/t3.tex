In Task 3, the modified DWA algorithm developed in previous tasks is deployed on a real TurtleBot3 to follow a moving target detected through visual landmarks in a laboratory environment. Three different experimental scenarios are conducted to assess the robustness of the controller under varying conditions. In these real scenarios, performance metrics are not directly comparable, as the robot trajectories differ and the target was manually moved by a human operator, preventing a consistent and accurate quantitative comparison, which a reproducible setting could yield.

A mutual property of all experiments is that the robot is able to compute candidate velocities when the dynamic goal is placed meters far from it. All the trajectories shown in Figures \ref{fig:r1_tr}, \ref{fig:r2a_tr}, and \ref{fig:r2b_tr} prove the reliability of the local planning algorithm, independently of the additional terms used in the objective functions.

The \texttt{/cmd\_vel} linear velocity profiles in plots \ref{fig:r1_cmd}, \ref{fig:r2a_cmd} and \ref{fig:r2b_cmd} give some insights on the differences introduced by the use of the additional terms in the objective functions. Rewarding slower velocities when the robot approaches the goal as in objective function 2a results in lower peaks (up to around 0.22m/s) compared to the ones in objective functions 1 and 2b. The linear velocities chosen by the adoption of objective function 2b have more frequent peaks, though always lower than those in obj. func. 1, also when the robot is close to the goal as a constant distance from it is required to be maintained.

It can be observed in \autoref{tab:metrics} that the success rate in the third real-world experiment is the lowest (4.08\%). This outcome is primarily due to the fact that, during the experiment, the robot did not remain sufficiently close to the target to trigger the goal-reached condition, while a greater emphasis is placed on testing collision-related behaviors.

For future analysis, a reproducible dynamic goal trajectory (and velocities) can be employed, in order to have a common ground goal among the three different adopted objective functions testing phases.
